I had a working app. Unit tests passed, the browser checks passed, Lighthouse gave it 100 for accessibility. Then I watched people take the drill on their own phones.

Every tester said the same thing first: it looked bad.

## The words were fine

Before that, I had read every sentence aloud to seven people who aren't developers, and nobody was confused by any of them. The words worked. The look didn't.

That was hard to hear, because I had treated the plain look as a feature. A safety tool, I thought, should look calm and serious. To the people using it, it looked unfinished, and an unfinished-looking tool about your money and your accounts is one you don't trust.

## What I changed

I rebuilt the whole interface. I mocked up three directions after browsing Refero Styles and picked the one I called Forest and lime. It uses system fonts only, so the app stayed small. Beyond colour, the user experience changed too:

- A **sticky progress bar** in the drill: "3 of 15 answered".
- A **big score** on the result, "1 of 5", with a five-part meter.
- A **"Your next step"** box with one button that jumps to the most urgent fix.
- **Plan steps as cards** that you can tick off as done.

Then I ran every check again on the new design, because a redesign is exactly when accessibility breaks. Zoom at 200% and 400%, 320-pixel screens, contrast in light and dark, keyboard only, colour removed, axe-core and Lighthouse: all passed, and so did TalkBack on my Samsung and VoiceOver on a Mac.

## What I got wrong

I didn't record the numbers. I don't have how many testers there were, their scores before and after, or how many hit the sign-in loop. The test log and the Devpost stories say so plainly, because a made-up number would be worse than none.

Next time I'll write the counts down as I go, even when the result is embarrassing. Especially then.

> People will tell you the look is wrong before they tell you anything else. For a tool people have to trust, the first impression is part of whether it works.
